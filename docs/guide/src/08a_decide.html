8Deciding where to go

This chapter answers the question you asked most directly: how does a robot in an unknown building choose its next target, and how do coverage patterns, team strategies and route planners fit together? The short answer: decisions are made in layers, and each layer answers one question.

8.1 Goals: the robot's intermediate targets

A robot never plans "search the whole building". It always has exactly one goal: a cell to reach and a reason to reach it. Reaching goals one after another is how the whole building gets covered. There are eight kinds:

The distance field

To compare goals a robot must know how far each one is by route, not in a straight line (a room behind a wall may be 1 m away but 30 m to drive). So at each decision the robot runs one Dijkstra search from its own cell to every cell of its map (a "distance field", mapping.dijkstra). One search gives the route length to all goals at once, which is why goal selection always uses Dijkstra, whatever route planner is chosen. For pattern waypoints, which may lie in unmapped space, a second, optimistic field treats unknown cells as passable (the free-space assumption).

When does a robot decide?

Deciding is not free, and changing one's mind every second makes robots dither. A robot chooses a new goal only when:

  • it has no goal, or has just reached it (and finished its scan or close look);
  • its goal became invalid: the frontier cell was searched by a teammate, the sighting was confirmed or rejected, the waypoint turned out to be inside an obstacle;
  • a new obstacle appeared on the next 20 cells of its route, or it has no route to its goal;
  • its battery says it must go home;
  • 12 seconds have passed since its last decision (a periodic re-check, because the map keeps changing).

8.2 The decision hierarchy

In simple words

First the robot asks "is there an emergency?" (battery, all found, news to deliver). If not, it asks its coverage pattern what to do next. With frontier exploration there is no fixed pattern, so it goes straight to the team strategy, which picks among the frontiers and sightings while taking teammates into account. Whatever is chosen, the route planner then works out the path. Finally the motion layer drives it, one cell at a time (chapter 10).
