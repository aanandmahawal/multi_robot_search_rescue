14How good is it? Measuring

A simulation is only useful if you can measure it honestly. The robots never see the ground truth, but the scoring code does: after every mission it checks every confirmed "person" against where people really are.

14.2 Reliability of the victim search (current rule)

scripts/reliability.py runs 12 missions per sensor set (six damaged building types × 2 seeds, 4 robots, 10 victims, 12 look-alikes, 6 warm objects) and classifies every confirmation and every rejection by what is really there:

SensorsVictims found / findableConfirmedRealCounted twiceFalse alarmsPrecisionSeconds robots disagreed
colour camera80 / 102937911385 %0
thermal camera99 / 1021189841683 %0
colour + thermal100 / 1021139921288 %0

What were the false alarms? With both cameras: a dog (5), sun-warmed rubble (3), nothing there (2), a jacket and a hot-water leak. With the thermal camera alone: mostly sun-warmed rubble (8). With the colour camera alone: mostly bags (8) and laptops (2). The colour camera misses all 18 people under thin debris; the thermal and fused detectors find every one of them, because heat leaks through. No sensor finds the 18 people under thick rubble, as designed.

How to read precision

88 % precision means roughly 1 in 9 people the fused team reports is not a person. For a real rescue that is a useful, honest number: the rescuers get a map where most flags are people, each with its measured temperature and how many robots saw it, and every flag is checked by a human before anyone digs.

14.3 Team strategies over 24 buildings

From the project's benchmark (4 buildings of each of the six types, seeds 200+, 4 robots, 900 s limit, perfect eyes so only teamwork is compared):

Partition and the auction are clearly better than greedy and far better than random. Partition drives least, because zones never overlap. The auction is as fast to find everyone and keeps working when zones are unequal. With perfect eyes all four strategies eventually find 100 % of detectable victims except random (86 %).

Source: results/vision/benchmark.md (python -m rescue benchmark). It was measured before the confirmation-rule change, which does not affect this perfect-eyes navigation comparison.

14.4 Navigation checks on the test ground

scripts/check_algorithms.py verifies the algorithms against known answers (chapter 9 has the planner table): all four planners return valid routes, Dijkstra and A* are exactly optimal, RRT* and the ants are within about 1 % on average. With 1 robot the lawnmower sweeps 4 lanes (108 m), reaches 75 of 77 waypoints, keeps within 0.21 m of its lanes on average and has searched 97.3 % when done; the spiral (2 rings, 95 m) reaches 67 of 68 waypoints, 0.29 m, 97.5 %. The frontier mop-up finishes the last few percent behind obstacles.
