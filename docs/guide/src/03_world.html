3The world: generating buildings

Every mission needs a building nobody has seen before, so that the robots truly explore. rescue/world.py builds one procedurally from a seed in about a tenth of a second: a floor plan, damage, furniture, rubble, victims and look-alikes, and then rescue/thermal.py gives every surface a temperature.

3.1 The pipeline

Step 1: the shell

The grid's left three columns (x = 0–2) are the staging area outside the building, where the robots and the base wait. An outer wall runs around the rest (x = 3 to 55, y = 0 to 39) and a 4-cell (2 m) wide entrance is cut into the left wall at mid-height. The rescuers know this outline (the size of the building), but nothing about its inside, which is exactly what the robots are allowed to know too.

Step 2: the layout of each building type

Each type has its own layout function (_layout_office, _layout_hospital, …). It draws interior walls with doors (hwall/vwall with 2-cell gaps at random positions), paints floor types (room, corridor, special area) and requests furniture. Rooms along a corridor are made by _rooms_along, which cuts a strip into rooms 8–11 cells wide with one door each onto the corridor.

Step 3: damage and collapse

The damage level (light / moderate / severe) sets three numbers: the share of interior walls that collapse (10 / 25 / 45 %), the rubble density (3 / 6 / 10 %) and the share of victims partly buried (20 / 40 / 60 %). Collapse picks random interior wall cells as starting points (share × wall cells / 6 of them) and removes a run of 2–4 wall cells along the wall from each. Each removed cell becomes rubble 0.4–1.2 m high with probability 35 %. So a collapsed wall can open a new shortcut, or it can fill a doorway with rubble.

Step 4: furniture that never seals off a room

This is the most important safety check in the generator. A randomly placed wardrobe could block the only door of a room, and then nobody could ever search that room. So every furniture request is tested before it is accepted:

reach  = flood_fill(blocked, from = entrance)        # cells reachable before
trial  = blocked + the furniture's cells
after  = flood_fill(trial, from = entrance)
accept only if  |after| == |reach| − (number of furniture cells)
# i.e. the furniture removed exactly its own cells from the reachable area, nothing more

Flood fill is a breadth-first search (BFS) that starts at the entrance and spreads to every free 4-neighbour. If adding the furniture made more cells unreachable than the furniture itself covers, it cut something off, and the request is dropped. The same test protects every victim, look-alike and warm object placed later (_try_place).

Step 5: rubble piles

Piles are grown as short random walks: from a random free cell, 2–6 steps in random directions, each visited cell getting rubble 0.3–1.4 m high. The number of piles is density × rubble slider × free cells / 4. Rubble is never placed on walls or furniture, and never within 4 cells of the entrance (the robots must be able to get in).

Step 6: people, look-alikes and the fine heightfield

A victim is a body 7 × 2 fine cells (1.75 m × 0.5 m) lying horizontally or vertically. It has a head, a torso in a shirt colour and legs in a trouser colour, and heights of about 0.2, 0.28 and 0.18 m. Victims are kept at least 2 m apart, and each placement must pass the same "cuts nothing off" test. Depending on the damage level, part of a body is covered by rubble 0.35–0.6 m thick (2–4 of its 7 segments). The thermal step later may bury some bodies completely (chapter 5).

Look-alikes (called decoys in the code) are things that can fool a colour camera: a jacket (flat, shirt-coloured), a bag, a cardboard box, a wooden beam and an orange barrel. Finally light floor debris (4 % of fine cells, 3–8 cm high) is scattered for visual clutter. Robots drive over it.

Where in the code

world.generate runs the pipeline; LAYOUTS maps each building name to its layout function; _flood is the flood fill; _try_place places bodies and objects without cutting anything off; _place_people_and_decoys builds bodies segment by segment. The world object (World) holds blocked, height, color, obj, temp, cover, the victims and the decoys.

3.2 The open test ground

The test ground (building plain) is deliberately not random. It is one empty hall with a 2 × 2 m block in the centre, two crates on the diagonal, 1.5 m cabinets in all four corners, shelves along the top and bottom walls and counters on the side walls, plus a faint painted 2 m grid. It has no collapse, rubble, look-alikes or warm objects. Only the victims change with the seed. That makes it the right place to check that a lawnmower really drives straight lanes, or that A* really finds the same route length as Dijkstra.

Interview tip: "how do you guarantee every area can be searched?"

"Every object is placed tentatively and accepted only if a flood fill from the entrance shows that the reachable area shrank by exactly the object's own cells. So no placement can seal off a room, and the metric 'share of reachable floor searched' always has a well-defined 100 %." Mention the seed too: the same seed reproduces the same building, which is what makes experiments fair.
