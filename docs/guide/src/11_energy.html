11Energy and batteries

A robot that runs flat in the middle of a collapsed building is a second rescue problem. So every move, every turn and every second of computing costs energy, and with a battery fitted the robots plan with their charge in mind: they refuse tasks they cannot come back from, turn home in time, and recharge.

Put together: a robot with both sensors, driving at 0.5 m/s, draws about 42 W, so 1 Wh (3,600 J) lasts about 85 seconds. Faster driving saves base energy (less time) but costs more in motor losses, which grow with speed².

Limitation: why the batteries on the dashboard are so small

A real 25 kg robot carries a few hundred Wh and works for hours. The simulated floor is only 28 × 20 m, and a mission takes minutes. So only batteries of 1–3 Wh run low within a mission, and the dashboard offers those sizes to let you watch the low-battery behaviour. The physics is the same; only the scale is shrunk.

11.2 Three battery rules

Rule 1: energy-aware task choice

For each candidate goal at route distance d, with dhome the route distance from that goal back to the base (from a distance field grown from the base on the robot's own map), the robot checks:

1.3 · (e · d + P · (d/v + 5 s))  +  1.3 · (e · dhome + P · dhome/v)  +  5 % of capacity  ≤  energy left

where e = 26.4 J/m is the driving energy per metre, P = 29 W the base power, v = 0.5 m/s, and 5 s the time for a scan at the goal. The 1.3 is a safety factor for detours and turns.

Worked example

A robot with a 1 Wh battery (3,600 J) has 60 % left: 2,160 J. A frontier is 6 m away, and from there the base is 12 m away.

  • Going there and scanning: 1.3 × (26.4 × 6 + 29 × (12 s + 5 s)) = 847 J
  • Coming back: 1.3 × (26.4 × 12 + 29 × 24 s) = 1,317 J
  • Reserve: 5 % × 3,600 = 180 J. Total needed: 2,344 J > 2,160 J.

So the robot does not take this frontier. If a nearer task fits, it takes that one. If none fits, it goes home to recharge: "7 task(s) left, but with 60 % battery I could not get there and back: going to recharge".

11.3 Per-robot batteries

Choosing Battery = Different per robot gives every robot its own capacity (1–10 Wh or unlimited). This models a mixed fleet, and it shows the energy-aware task choice at work: robots with more charge take the far tasks, and robots with less charge take near tasks or recharge. Each robot's name tag in the 3-D view shows its charge ("R1 · 62%", amber below 45 %, red below 20 %, ⚡ while charging), and the Robots tab lists capacity, charge, trips to the charger and time spent charging.

Interview tip: "how does battery affect task allocation?"

"Each robot filters its candidate tasks by feasibility: energy to get there and do the work, plus energy to get back home, both with a 1.3 safety factor, plus a 5 % reserve, must fit in what is left. So in the auction a low-battery robot simply does not bid on far tasks, and a better-charged teammate wins them. The energy model is physical (rolling resistance, motor losses, turning, base power), and the distances come from route distance fields, not straight lines."
