15Honest limitations

Every simulation simplifies. Knowing exactly where this one does, and what a real system would need instead, is a sign of understanding, and interviewers often ask. Here is the complete list.

Why these simplifications are reasonable

The project studies decisions: how to explore, how to split work, how to plan and recover, how to turn noisy detections into reliable reports. Each simplification removes a problem that has its own large literature (SLAM, motion control, sim-to-real) without changing the decision logic that sits on top of it. A useful sentence for an interview is: "The navigation and coordination logic would run unchanged on top of a real localisation and control stack; what would change is where the map and the pose come from."

15.1 What is deliberately realistic

  • Robots know nothing about the inside of the building: they discover it with their own noisy sensors.
  • The LiDAR is blind below its scan plane, and the camera is blocked by tall furniture. Both limits shape the search.
  • Some victims cannot be found by any camera (thick rubble), and some look-alikes fool each sensor. Results report both instead of hiding them.
  • Every move costs time according to its length and turn, and energy according to a physical model.
  • Every result is checked against the ground truth on missions not used for tuning.

15.2 Natural next steps

  1. Add pose noise and a simple scan-matching localiser, and measure how map quality and search time degrade.
  2. Replace the central auction with message-based bidding and a lossy radio.
  3. Add a sound sensor (calling victims) and fuse it with the thermal evidence in the belief filter.
  4. Inflate obstacles by the robot's radius and follow routes with pure pursuit, for continuous motion.
  5. Train the detector with domain randomisation, then test it on real thermal images.
