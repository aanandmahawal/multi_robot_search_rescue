5Heat: how temperatures are assigned

A thermal camera is only as meaningful as the temperatures it looks at. rescue/thermal.py gives every 25 cm square of the building a surface temperature in °C, following simple physical rules. The rules are chosen so that the thermal camera faces the same difficulties as a real one: warm things that are not people, and people it cannot see.

5.1 The building itself

Each building type has an ambient temperature: office 20 °C, apartments 21, hospital 22, school 19, parking 13, warehouse 15, test ground 20. Collapsed walls let outside air in, so damage cools it: −0.5 °C (light), −1.5 °C (moderate), −3 °C (severe). On top of that:

  • drift: a smooth random field (Gaussian-blurred noise, σ = 6 fine cells = 1.5 m) scaled to ±0.8 °C, for draughts and sunlit patches;
  • noise: ±0.15 °C per square;
  • walls and structure are 0.6 °C cooler (concrete and steel).

5.2 People

Each victim gets a skin temperature drawn between 33 and 36 °C. Half of the victims have been trapped for hours and have cooled by up to 4 °C (hypothermia), so a victim can be as cool as 29 °C. The head segment shows the skin temperature. Torso and legs are covered by clothing, which insulates and shows 4–7 °C cooler than the skin.

5.3 Rubble blocks the heat

This rule decides which victims can be found at all. Heat leaking through a cover of thickness d falls off exponentially:

How many victims are fully covered depends on the damage: light 5 % thin / 0 % thick, moderate 10 % / 10 %, severe 10 % / 20 %. People under thick rubble are undetectable by design. The project reports them separately ("victims buried deep") so its results never pretend that a camera can see through concrete. Finding them would need listening devices, search dogs or rescuers digging.

5.4 Warm things that are not people

A colour camera can be fooled by a jacket; a thermal camera by anything warm. Six warm objects per building (chosen to suit the building type) give the thermal detector realistic hard cases:

5.5 The one physics rule the robots use

Nothing on a living person is hotter than about 40 °C. So if the detector says "person" but the hottest pixel of that detection is above 40 °C, the detection is dropped as a heater or an engine (thermal_check, HUMAN_MAX). This rule cannot remove a dog or warm rubble, because they sit inside the human range. Those are left to the neural network (shape and appearance) and the belief filter (repeated, close looks), chapter 7.

Interview tip: "is the temperature real?"

"No. It is a physically-motivated simulated temperature field: ambient per building, skin 33–36 °C with possible hypothermia, clothing 4–7 °C cooler, heat through rubble falling as e−d/10 cm, and warm look-alikes with realistic ranges. A camera model then adds blur, noise, drift and haze. The point is that the thermal detector has to cope with the same ambiguities a real one does: a dog at 33 °C, and a person under 50 cm of concrete with no signature at all."

Where in the code

rescue/thermal.py: add_thermal (ambient, drift, walls), _bury_and_warm_victims (skin, clothing, covers, heat leak), _place_warm_objects, _warm_car_engines, palette (ironbow colours). It uses its own random stream (seed + 7), so adding heat never changed any building layout.
